## 4. Conditional affiliate sensitivity, not the project base case

Let **M** be qualified monthly active users, **c** attributable merchant clicks per user per month, **v** placed-order conversion per click, **a** the fraction of placed orders finally approved, **V** eligible net order value, and **r** the commission rate:

**Monthly gross accrued commission = M × c × v × a × V × r.**

“Qualified” means an eligible, approved distribution and attribution path, not every install. All numbers below are deliberately unvalidated hypotheses, including prices and rates. Low/base/high combine changes in traffic and unit economics; they are sensitivity examples, not probabilities or demand estimates.

| Input or calculated output | Conditional low | Conditional base | Conditional high |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| M: qualified MAU | 100 | 1,000 | 10,000 |
| c: attributable clicks/user/month | 0.25 | 0.50 | 0.75 |
| v: placed orders/click | 2% | 3% | 5% |
| a: approved fraction | 70% | 80% | 85% |
| V: eligible net order value, PKR | 5,000 | 6,000 | 8,000 |
| r: hypothetical commission | 2% | 3% | 4% |
| Attributable clicks/month | 25 | 500 | 7,500 |
| Expected placed orders/month | 0.50 | 15 | 375 |
| Expected approved orders/month | 0.35 | 12 | 318.75 |
| **Gross accrued commission/month, PKR** | **35** | **2,160** | **102,000** |
| Gross commission/MAU/month, PKR | 0.35 | 2.16 | 10.20 |

Fractional orders are expected-value arithmetic, not observed purchases. Apply cancellation, rejected COD and returns **once through a**. Define V from the actual agreement's eligible amount, excluding non-commissionable components; do not apply a second generic returns discount to approved revenue. Partial refunds need their actual eligible-value adjustment.

Accrued commission is not cash received. Report pending, approved, reversed and paid amounts separately. Until the payout threshold, validation period, settlement schedule, bank route and deductions are known, cash arrival cannot be forecast. The conditional base earns PKR 180 per approved order, PKR 4.32 per attributable click and PKR 2.16 per qualified MAU. Covering a hypothetical PKR 5,000 monthly cash expense would require about 2,315 such MAU, before tax and labor. The expense is illustrative, not a provider quote.

Cash contribution is approved accrued revenue less variable cash costs; profit also needs fixed costs and labor. Neither is established by the gross figures.

## 6. Conditional load model and provider limits

This model concerns a later licensed catalog service. **U means daily active users for load, distinct from M, the revenue MAU.** No DAU/MAU ratio or cohort relationship is assumed.

Assume three local watch-interest equivalents per U, with 40% unique offers: **N = 1.2U**. These are demand proxies, not uploaded watches; the server has no user identity or watch table. It maintains a curated licensed corpus. Inactive users, uneven demand and catalog breadth could invalidate this ratio.

Two accepted raw observation slots per offer per day give **O = 2.4U/day**. Re-reading an unchanged merchant source-generation timestamp without a new independently timestamped validity assertion updates only a check heartbeat; it must not create fresh observed history. A daily comparison aggregate uses at most one sample/day. The arithmetic conservatively assumes both daily slots produce acceptable new snapshots. Retain at most 90 days, or less where the grant requires it.

Published Free constraints relevant here are Workers 100,000 incoming requests/day, **10 ms CPU per HTTP or Cron invocation**, 15-minute Cron wall time, five Cron definitions and 128 MB memory [T16]. Network waiting is not CPU; a 15-minute wall allowance does not provide 15 minutes of Free computation. D1 allows 5 million rows read and 100,000 rows written/day [T17], with **500 MB per database**, 50 queries per Worker invocation and 100 bound parameters per statement [T18]. These are ceilings, not throughput evidence.

Assume one viewer workflow/user/day: **two API calls**, one for offers and one for a selected offer's history. Request a sufficient bounded history limit; extra pagination, histories or reopened views add multipliers. Incoming work is **2U + B + P + 10**, where **B = ceil(O/10)** operator ingestion batches and **P = ceil(O/100)** expiry batches. An authenticated operator dispatcher is proposed, not implemented. Source acquisition is separately consolidated: at most two authorized snapshots/day/source, approximately 2F fetches for F feeds, rather than O product-page fetches. Never substitute always-on scraping.

Ten-record ingestion batches and 100-row expiry batches require CPU, query-count and payload profiling. Splitting records does not remove account quotas. No unselected queue, cron dispatcher or paid service is silently included.

## 7. Rows, indexes, retention and traffic

The reference schema has offers, observations, variants and provenance/access records. Observations use an integer primary key, one unique `(offer_id, collection_slot_utc)` index and one `observed_at` retention index. Other unindexed provenance/revision fields remain in row storage; see [contracts](06-data-and-api-contracts.md).

**ASSUMPTION:** each accepted slot costs one current-offer update plus observation table/index insertion: **1 + 3 = 4 writes**. After 90 days, deleting one expired observation adds three table/index writes: **7 writes/slot**. Add 10% reserve for corrections and maintenance: warmup **10.56U** and mature **18.48U writes/day**. Initial offer/variant setup adds **5.28U writes** separately. Changing indexed offer fields or adding indexes raises this budget. Deletes are not free; D1 counts scanned rows and index maintenance, and exposes actual metadata for validation [T17].

Assume **400 scanned rows across the two-call workflow** for bounded history, index traversal and metadata; ingestion uses four reads/slot, expiry three, access checks two/batch, and operator work 100/day. Thus normal reads are **400U + 7O + 2B + 100**. This is a query-cost hypothesis, not a proven bound. A weekly independent export adds **220U reads on its actual backup day**; daily averaging would hide a quota breach.

Storage assumes **1 KiB (1,024 bytes)/observation including its two indexes**, **2 KiB (2,048 bytes)/unique offer** for ancillary rows, then 25% page/fragmentation overhead. The rich JSON contracts make a small compact-record estimate inappropriate; these larger values still require measurement:

**90-day bytes = 1.25 × [(90 × 2.4U × 1,024) + (1.2U × 2,048)] = 279,552U.**

Use decimal MB conservatively against 500,000,000 bytes. No real schema-size or query-plan measurement exists. Grant records, long URLs and payloads must fit the ancillary allowance or trigger recalculation.

## 8. First modeled bottleneck and stop rules

Under these assumptions, **90-day D1 storage reaches the first quantified long-run ceiling at approximately 1,788 DAU**. Mature writes reach their ceiling near 5,411 and backup-day reads near 7,845. The 10,000-DAU case exceeds warmup writes, mature writes, storage and export-day reads. It is not feasible on this proposed Free configuration.

This ordering is conditional. Doubling observation size to **2 KiB**, retaining the same ancillary allowance, produces **556,032U bytes** and moves the storage ceiling to roughly **899 DAU**; even 1,000 users then exceed 500 MB. Raising unique offer demand also increases storage and writes proportionally. CPU could fail first even at 100 users; no request latency, ingestion duration or sustainable throughput has been tested.

Warn at **80%** of each quota or projected 90-day storage and freeze new corpus growth. The baseline storage warning occurs near **1,430 DAU**. Stop application ingestion at **90%** to retain headroom for expiry and administration: about **1,609 DAU** for baseline storage. Provider limits at 100% remain hard ceilings. D1 daily read/write exhaustion can block queries until reset [T17]. Public reads also need bounded responses, caching and rate/abuse controls; ordinary demand arithmetic does not cover attacks.

At exhaustion or outage, show dated unavailable/stale data, stop freshness claims and retain the local notebook. Freeze ingestion or temporarily disable the cloud feature; never fall back to raw retailer fetching, free proxies, quota-evasion sharding or automatic paid upgrades. Do not refresh timestamps to hide missed checks. License-mandated server expiry is planned retention, separate from P0's user-controlled local cleanup.
